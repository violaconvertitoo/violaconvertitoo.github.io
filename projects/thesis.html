HERTIE SCHOOL

Master’s Thesis – Women’s Empowerment and Food Security in Syria

Empirical analysis of 932 female-headed households in Syria examining empowerment, productive resources and food security.

ROLSHousehold surveyGenderFood security

How do marital status, land ownership and education shape the relationship between women’s empowerment and food security in conflict-affected Syria?

Research question & motivation

Female household headship does not automatically imply empowerment. This thesis examines whether women’s access to productive resources and their marital status are associated with household food security in government-controlled areas of Syria.

Research design in practice

The project connects three dimensions of empowerment—resources, agency and achievements—to measurable household food-security outcomes. I constructed indicators from survey modules, harmonized household characteristics and estimated pooled OLS specifications for the Food Consumption Score (FCS), Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS) and Reduced Coping Strategies Index (RCSI).

Inputs2023 baseline survey; female-headed households; household characteristics, assets and food-security modules
ProcessingMissingness checks, recoding, indicator construction and sample definition
ModelsPooled OLS with household-clustered standard errors, controls and interaction terms
OutputsRegression tables, subgroup comparisons and policy-relevant interpretation

Why the findings matter

The analysis highlights that greater household decision-making responsibility does not necessarily coincide with better food-security outcomes. The role of productive resources, including land, motivates attention to material constraints as well as agency.

Data & empirical strategy

The study uses survey data from 932 female-headed households across five Syrian governorates. Food security is measured using the Food Consumption Score (FCS), Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS) and Reduced Coping Strategy Index (RCSI). Nine pooled OLS specifications examine main effects and interactions between marital status and land ownership or education, with household-clustered standard errors.

Selected findings

  • Widowed female household heads reported greater decision-making authority but also higher food insecurity.
  • Access to land was associated with improved food-security outcomes, particularly among widowed women.
  • Education offered less consistent protection in the context studied, highlighting structural constraints beyond individual characteristics.

What I developed

Designed the research framework and hypotheses; prepared survey data and food-security indicators; implemented descriptive analysis and OLS regressions in R; interpreted interaction terms and model limitations; and wrote the final thesis.

Interpretation & limitations

The analysis is observational. Estimated relationships should not be interpreted as causal effects. The survey sample and conflict context also limit generalization. Underlying FAO/ISDC data cannot be redistributed.

Research outputs

The thesis includes descriptive comparisons, regression tables and interaction analyses. Selected figures can be added after verifying that their release is authorized. Cleaned public-facing code is being prepared.
