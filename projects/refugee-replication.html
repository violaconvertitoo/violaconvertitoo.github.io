HERTIE SCHOOL

Replication Assignment – Refugee Exposure and Political Attitudes

Group replication of an instrumental-variables study examining the effects of refugee exposure on attitudes.

RIVReplicationMigrationCausal inference

A group replication of published research examining whether exposure to refugee arrivals changes attitudes toward refugees.

Original study & research question

This academic assignment engages with the identification strategy in Does Exposure to the Refugee Crisis Make Natives More Hostile? by Hangartner and co-authors (2019). It asks how exposure to refugee arrivals affects host-population attitudes.

Identification strategy and critical assessment

The original paper studies whether exposure to refugee arrivals affected native residents’ attitudes. Our group examined the study’s instrumental-variables design, in which geographic proximity to the Turkish coast is used to isolate variation in exposure. The key question is whether the instrument predicts refugee arrivals while influencing attitudes only through that exposure.

Research designQuasi-experimental instrumental variables
First stageRelationship between geographic instrument and refugee exposure
Second stageEstimated association between instrumented exposure and attitudes
Critical assessmentInstrument relevance, exclusion restriction, contextual confounding and interpretation

This is a group coursework replication, not an independently conducted field experiment. The full assignment documents the group's analysis; individual contributions should be credited precisely when publishing code.

What the replication demonstrates

Beyond estimating a coefficient, the assignment requires reconstructing a published empirical design, interpreting the instrument and discussing the assumptions that make causal claims credible. The original study’s findings should not be attributed to the replication team as new discoveries.

Empirical strategy

The replication focuses on an instrumental-variables approach that uses geographic variation associated with distance to the Turkish coast and refugee arrival patterns. The core methodological task is to evaluate the relevance of the instrument and the assumptions required for a causal interpretation.

Replication workflow

  • Reconstruct the selected published analysis and compare the estimated pattern with the original study.
  • Inspect the IV specification, first-stage logic and threats to the exclusion restriction.
  • Discuss identification assumptions, robustness and interpretation rather than treating a statistically significant coefficient as automatically causal.

What this demonstrates

Applied experience reading and reproducing published empirical research, implementing instrumental variables in R and critically assessing causal identification.

Collaboration & access

Completed as a three-person group assignment. The original paper belongs to its authors. A reviewed public version of the assignment and collaborator acknowledgements will be linked after approval; no unverified numerical replication results are claimed here.
